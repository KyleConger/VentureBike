# External Research Synthesis (Advisory)

Reviewed ~10 minutes of public Marketplace / Venture Bikes write-ups (WGU C216 community notes, Marketplace product pages, student reports, strategy blogs). **Treat as heuristics, not gospel** — demand, costs, and willingness-to-pay are **cohort-specific**. Always overwrite with your in-sim market survey.

## What consistently correlates with strong BSC finishes

1. **Brand–segment fit is the #1 lever**  
   Design features to the segment’s stated needs (comfort/utility for Recreation; rugged/power for Mountain; light/aero for Speed). Misaligned brands lose share even with heavy ads.

2. **Ads must mirror features + motivators**  
   Strong teams use ~5–6 ad attributes: brand name → motivator → key features (tires, seat, etc.). Weak ads are a common early BSC drag. **Match or exceed top competitor ad quality** (aligns with team goal).

3. **Pay above average**  
   Compensation above industry average improves HR scores, productivity, and reduces churn — matches your explicit goal.

4. **Capacity leads demand by ~1 quarter**  
   Fixed capacity (printers) has lag. Under-capacity → lost sales; over-capacity → asset/financial risk. Forecast from 12-month potential ÷ quarters, then stress-test.

5. **Build-to-order / lean inventory**  
   The scenario rewards not tying cash in stock; schedule production to demand, watch overtime vs lost sales tradeoff.

6. **R&D from Q4 onward is mandatory for late-game**  
   Enriched materials / new features keep brands competitive when everyone piles into the same segments.

7. **Play the scorecard, not only “real world intuition”**  
   Balanced scorecard weights marketing effectiveness, HR, future investment, and risk — not just net income.

## City sequencing patterns (for your 1/quarter rule)

Observed approaches:

| Approach | Order | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| Demand-first | Often NY / Amsterdam early | Capture largest potentials sooner |
| Cost-first | Bangalore first | Lower open/op costs; stretch Q1–Q2 cash |
| Hybrid | High-demand #1, then next-best demand | Balance share vs cost |

**Team constraint**: one new city each quarter, all four open by Q4.  
**Draft recommendation pending survey**: 

1. **Q1** — highest combined demand city for your chosen primary segment (often New York or Amsterdam in public samples)  
2. **Q2** — second-best demand / complementary demographics  
3. **Q3** — next  
4. **Q4** — remaining city

## Channel consensus

- Open a **web sales center** early (Q1) if the sim allows — supports “digital + physical” goal and incremental demand without full store cost.  
- Still open physical shops on the city cadence; web does not replace the expansion score/path.

## Segment strategy under “all three segments”

Public tips often say “pick 1–2 primary.” Your team overrides that: **enter all three**. Practical compromise used by high-performing all-segment teams:

- **Q1**: Design **one brand per segment** (3 brands) OR primary + secondary with third locked for Q2 — prefer **3 brands in Q1** if the UI allows, so Q2 can price/ads/hire against a full assortment.  
- Focus media & sales priority on the segment with best margin × demand, but do not orphan any segment’s brand design.  
- Scale to 2 brands/segment only after Q3–Q4 when cash/R&D allow (avoid spreading ads too thin early).

## Capacity growth examples from paid tip sites (illustrative only)

Some guides cite fixed capacity ramps like: Q2 ~16–24 → Q3 ~30 → Q4 ~48–60 → Q5 ~90–120 → Q6 ~180–230. **Do not copy blindly** — size to *your* forecast + overtime policy + cash floor.

## Common failure modes

- Ignoring market research after Q1  
- Ads that don’t match bike features  
- Underpaying staff → productivity/HR collapse  
- Opening cities too slowly (you won’t) or opening without salespeople/ads  
- Starving R&D in Q4–Q5  
- Burning cash with no emergency reserve (you have an explicit cushion rule)  
- Overproducing relative to demand early, or underproducing in growth quarters
