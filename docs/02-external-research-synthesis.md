## What consistently correlates with strong BSC finishes

1. **Brand–segment fit is the #1 lever**  
   Design features to the segment’s stated needs (comfort/utility for Recreation; rugged/power for Mountain; light/aero for Speed). Misaligned brands lose share even with heavy ads. **Truth-in-advertising** matters — claiming lights/reflectors/comfort seat you don’t have risks Brand Judgment *and* deceptive-ad flags.

2. **Ads must mirror features + motivators**  
   Strong teams use ~**5–6** ad attributes (not the max 9): brand name → motivator → key features (tires, seat, etc.). Weak ads are a common early BSC drag. **Match or exceed top competitor ad quality**.  
   SERP tip (Workspace): search results show only the **first three benefits** — rank those carefully. Design **≥2 test ads** (primary + secondary; optional company ad).

3. **Pay above average**  
   Compensation above industry average improves HR scores, productivity, and retention. Early quarters: employees push hardest on **salary**, then health → vacation → pension. Interactive planning: package vs ~**70%** productivity norm.

4. **Capacity leads demand by ~1 quarter**  
   Fixed capacity (printers) has lag. Under-capacity → lost sales + **ill-will** (next-Q demand cut by **½ of unmet %**); over-capacity → idle labor/OH and cash drain. Stress-test demand ±50%.

5. **Build-to-order / lean inventory**  
   Make-to-order; no warehouse stock. Schedule OC to demand; use OT as safety net. Brands Marketing prices auto-enter production.

6. **R&D from Q4 onward is mandatory for late-game**  
   Enriched materials / new features when everyone piles into the same segments.

7. **Play the scorecard, not only “real world intuition”**  
   BSC weights marketing effectiveness, HR, future investment, wealth, manufacturing productivity — not just NI.

## Pricing — historical consensus (important)

**Alumni / tip-site pattern is value-based (WTP), not a fixed cost-plus markup.**

| Heuristic | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| Primary anchor | **Price Willing to Pay** (ideal-brand ceiling) from survey |
| Typical posture | **At or slightly under WTP** if Brand/Ad Judgment strong; slightly lower if fighting share |
| Cost’s role | **Floor / contribution check** + pro forma — not the pricing formula |
| “~30% over cost” | **Not** the historical winning rule; underprices Mountain once volume drops unit cost; can still lose money early after opex |
| Rec vs Mtn/Speed | Rec more price-sensitive (rebates sometimes help); value-driven segments often **hurt** by rebates |

### Alumni ballpark list prices (verify every cohort)

| Segment | Common starting ballpark | Notes |
|---------|--------------------------|--------|
| Recreation | ~$1,000–$1,050 (+ small rebate) | Most price-sensitive |
| Mountain | ~$1,260–$1,500 | Premium for capability |
| Speed | ~$1,480–$1,800 | Highest WTP; protect contribution |

**WeBike survey ceilings (this cohort):** Rec **$1,100** · Mountain **$1,365** · Speed **$1,580**.  
Hike Bike early COGS (Workspace): ~$924 @100u → ~$675 @250u → ~$550 @500u — same “+30%” rule yields very different strategy quality at each volume.

## Dual brands / “two mountain products”

| Pattern | Historical consensus |
|---------|----------------------|
| Early (Q1–Q2) | **One brand per target segment**; don’t dual-brand inside Mountain yet |
| Mid/late | **2nd brand in same segment** once ads/sales/R&D can support both (share play) |
| Tip-site endgame | Sometimes 2–3 brands per segment |
| Cross-segment “mountain bike for Rec/Speed” | **Fails** — Brand Judgment is segment-specific |
| Too-early dual Mountain | Public tip blogs note poor customer evaluation until designs/ads fixed |

For WeBike: dual Mountain is a **later share play**, not a Q2 substitute for Speed/Rec.

## Segment entry patterns (external vs this team)

Public tips often say “pick 1–2 primary.” Tip-sheet “winners” frequently Rec/volume- or Speed-first.  
**This team:** Mountain → Speed → Recreation; “profit margin leader” is **intent** until COGS/pro forma prove it.

Practical compromise used by high-performing all-segment teams:

- Design **one brand per segment** as capacity allows; don’t orphan designs forever.  
- Focus media & sales on best margin × demand first.  
- Scale to 2 brands/segment only after Q3–Q4 when ads/sales can support.

## Channel consensus

- Open **web + physical** early (instructor favors both; online-only limits growth).  
- Web: broader reach; harder to segment except via ads; needs phone/email + fitting help; **diminishing returns** on web headcount.  
- Web productivity tactics (toll-free, page upgrades) start this quarter but take **a few quarters** to mature; **diminishing returns** on budget (most gain by ~$10k; toll-free >> page upgrades on Workspace improvement curves).  
- Organic SEM first (slow); support with traditional ads that list **brand + URL**; paid SEM later. Keywords = agency set by **segment**, not ad benefit bullets.

## Advertising & media (historical + Workspace)

- ≥2 ads in test market; ≤9 benefits (optimum often ~5–6).  
- Media preference is **segment-specific** (this cohort one-time report):  
  - **Mountain:** Biking → Sport → General News (avoid Leisure — Rec-skewed)  
  - **Speed:** Biking → Business → Sport  
  - **Recreation:** Leisure → Health & Fitness → General News

## Manufacturing / capacity heuristics

| Topic | Consensus / Workspace |
|-------|------------------------|
| Fixed vs OC | OC ≤ fixed; printers ordered **this** Q available **next** Q |
| Productivity pad | Early ~**70%** → inflate OC by ~**30%** (Demand/65 × 1.30) |
| Labor vs OC curve | **U-shaped** labor: trough ~**18–22**/day; rises near fixed max (**24**) |
| Overhead vs OC | Falls then flats (~$45–50 at mid/high OC) |
| Overtime | 0–4 h/day; 2× pay; productivity **70% → 64.4%** at 4h; safety net vs excess capacity |
| Stock-outs | Ill-will: next Q demand − **½ of unmet %** |
| Capacity ramps (tip sites) | e.g. Q2 ~16–24 → Q3 ~30 → … — **illustrative only**; size to your forecast |

## Common failure modes

- Cost-plus pricing that ignores WTP (or WTP pricing that ignores cash/COGS floor)  
- Ads that don’t match bike features / deceptive claims  
- Dual brands or multi-segment ads too early → thin Ad Judgment  
- Underpaying staff → productivity/HR collapse  
- Staffing/producing to **naive potential÷4** in Q2  
- Running OC at fixed max when labor U-curve is rising  
- Ignoring stock-out ill-will or excess capacity costs  
- Opening cities without salespeople/ads  
- Starving R&D in Q4–Q5  
- Burning cash below emergency floor (**Cash+CD ≥ $300k**)
