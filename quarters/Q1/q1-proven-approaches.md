# Quarter 1 — Proven Approaches (Research Brief)

**Status:** Advisory only. **No team decisions are locked in this file.**

## What Q1 is (and is not)

Q1 is **company setup**, not test-market execution.

| Typically in Q1 | Typically starts Q2 |
|-----------------|---------------------|
| Name company | Prices & brand priority |
| Organize team / responsibilities | Ad copy & media campaigns |
| Review market research | Sales staffing & internet tactics |
| Corporate goals & strategic direction | Compensation packages |
| Design ≥1 brand per target segment | Production schedule by brand |
| Open store and/or web sales center | Heavy demand fulfillment |
| Set up production facility | Iteration from competitive feedback |

You get **feedback after** later quarters process. Q1’s job is to create the **resources** (RBV) you will operate in Q2+.

Knowledge checks frame ~$300k as the minimum to keep in financial projections to reduce emergency-loan risk. Your team goal (“emergency cash”) may set a **higher** internal floor—but **$300k Cash+CD is the non-negotiable sim floor**.

**Finance stress-test tip (from Marketplace exam materials):** after decisions, run production/pro forma scenarios (e.g., demand well below plan) and reload cash flow to confirm the ending Cash+CD balance still clears the floor.

Capital this week (class framing): **+$1,500,000 angel** available to spend on setup. Spend productively (team goal) without breaching the $300k Cash+CD rule.

## Decision area: Company name

**What “proven” looks like:** Memorable, professional, bike/carbon/tech-adjacent; easy to use in ads later. Name quality rarely decides BSC; **speed of agreement** matters more than wordsmithing.

**Do not lock a name here** — team chooses in session.

## Decision area: Organize team & corporate responsibilities

**What tends to work:**

- Map owners to tactical areas: Marketing, Sales, Manufacturing, Accounting/Finance, Business Analytics, HR, Leadership.  
- With five people, combine seats (e.g., Sales+HR; Analytics shared with Finance or Marketing).  
- Agree who has final say on cash-floor compliance (usually Finance) and who owns brand design quality (usually Marketing).

**RBV angle:** clear decision rights are an organizational capability—reduces last-minute uncoordinated changes (a known failure mode in knowledge-check scenarios).

## Decision area: Review market research

**What high-performing teams do in Q1:**

1. Rank **customer needs/wants** per segment (drives brand components).  
2. Note **price willing to pay** per segment (used in Q2 pricing—still inform Q1 “can we profitably serve all three?”).  
3. Compare **12-month market potential** by city × segment (informs **which city opens first**).  
4. Rough quarterly demand heuristic used in training materials:  
   `quarterly potential ≈ 12-month potential / 4`  
   (Crude; refine later with purchased research and results.)

Buy extra research only if free data leaves a critical gap **and** Cash+CD still clears $300k.

## Decision area: Brand design (≥1 per target segment)

**Most consistent “proven” Q1 insight across sources:**

> **Brand design is a primary long-run driver.** Features must match segment need rankings. Misaligned bikes get poor **Brand Judgment**, which drags **Marketing Effectiveness** even before ads.

Practical notes from Marketplace materials / debriefs:

- Design to the survey, not to teammate taste.  
- Recreation ≠ Mountain ≠ Speed on comfort vs ruggedness vs weight/aero.  
- Weak recreation designs have drawn consumer-union style penalties when minimum expectations are missed.  
- Brand Judgment in the ~80 range is cited as solid positioning; very low scores undermine sellability.  
- For an all-segment strategy, Q1 commonly creates **one brand per segment** (three brands). Adding a second brand per segment is usually a **later** move when ads/sales capacity can support it.

**No feature list or brand names are chosen in this brief.**

## Decision area: Bike store and/or web sales center

**Channel evidence:**

- Instructor: **don’t be online-only**; physical decisions matter for growth.  
- Team goal + instructor: **both** web and a physical outlet are the default planning assumption—**team must still formally choose**.  
- Knowledge-check note: web sales center staffing needs are often **higher** than a single bricks store (matters in Q2 hiring, not Q1 payroll).

**First city — observed patterns (options, not a pick):**

| Pattern | Typical first city logic | Tradeoff |
|---------|--------------------------|----------|
| Demand-first | Highest potential for primary/all segments (often NY or Amsterdam in public samples) | Higher open/lease cost |
| Cost-first | Lower-cost city (often Bangalore in student reports) | May delay peak demand capture |
| Balanced | High demand with moderate cost (Amsterdam appears often in debriefs) | Depends entirely on *your* survey |

Your constraint: open **exactly one** new city in Q1 if following “one city per quarter.” Remaining three cities: Q2, Q3, Q4.

## Decision area: Production facility

**What Q1 facility decisions are really about:**

- Creating **fixed capacity** (3D printers / facility configuration) that will support Q2+ test demand.  
- Fixed capacity expansions often have **lead time** (invest in one quarter, available in a later quarter)—so undersizing early can bottleneck growth.  
- Oversizing burns cash and can hurt later productivity/asset scores if idle.  
- Competitive-advantage questions in training materials ask teams to pick Q1 fixed-capacity investment levels **consistent with strategy** (cost leadership vs differentiation vs broad coverage)—match spend to your all-segment, fast-geographic plan without violating Cash+CD ≥ $300k.

**Rough planning loop used by strong teams:**

1. Estimate early demand from survey potentials (and city #1 + web).  
2. Size facility/printers for near-term quarters, remembering lead times.  
3. Re-check Cash+CD ≥ $300k after facility + store + web costs.  
4. Prefer leaving optional CD balance as part of the $300k cushion if the sim allows.

**No printer count or dollar facility size is locked here.**

## Common Q1 mistakes to avoid

1. Leaving Cash+CD under **$300k**.  
2. Designing brands without reading need scores.  
3. Web-only (limits growth; conflicts with instructor + team goals).  
4. Opening multiple cities in Q1 (breaks your one-per-quarter plan; also cash-heavy).  
5. Spending so little that Q2 lacks capacity/channel resources (“maximize spend” opposite failure).  
6. Treating Q1 as pricing/ads quarter and rushing past brand–segment fit.  
7. Making last-minute capacity changes without the full team (cited failure pattern in exam scenarios).
