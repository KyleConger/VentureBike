## Channel model

Sell only through **company-operated** bicycle shops and an optional **web sales center** (not independent multi-brand retailers).

**Why own the channel**

- Full control of brand selection, pricing, advertising, and sales  
- No point-of-sale surprises from independents  
- Freedom to adjust as you learn customers, competition, and local costs  

**Timing rule**

- A bike store or web sales center takes a **full quarter** to open  
- Schedule the first channel opening in **Q1** to test-market in **Q2**

## Brick-and-mortar

**Advantages:** touch/feel/test ride; segment-specialist sales; custom fit; local control.  
**Disadvantages:** expensive; many details.

**Store setup:** ~3,200 sq ft / 300 m²; middle-class area near major road; display ~50%, fitting ~20%, service ~15%, other ~15%.  
**Productivity risk:** more than ~15 display bikes or ~15 salespeople likely reduces productivity.

**Team posture:** Amsterdam shop in Q1 — **LOCKED**.

---

## Web sales center

**Advantages:** one site for all geos; broader metro reach; multilingual staff.  
**Disadvantages:** no touch/feel; weaker phone vs in-person; must be found via ads/SEM; staff wait for calls.

**Fulfillment:** web order → fab facility → 3D specs → next-day build → ship to store pickup or break-bulk if no store.

**Team posture:** Web in Q1 — **UNDECIDED** (yellow cell in `Q1Data.xlsx`).

---

## Sim decision tips

1. Launching **both** channels at once is expensive — may start with one.  
2. Channels **cannibalize** each other but may raise **total** demand.  
3. Segment shopping prefs differ (Rec wants humans **and** web — see Needs).  
4. Recommended test path: **one store** first.

> **Human call — web in Q1?**  
> Team goal favors digital + physical. Sim warns cost + cannibalization. Decide vs **Cash + CD ≥ $300,000**.
