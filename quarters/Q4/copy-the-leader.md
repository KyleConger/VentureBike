BB LLC scored **22** on the Q3 quarterly scorecard. Next best was SpaceBikes at 11. We scored **0**.

More usefully, BB LLC targets **the same two segments we do** — Mountain primary, Speed secondary. SpaceBikes leads cumulatively but skips Mountain entirely, so only their manufacturing model transfers. BB LLC transfers wholesale.

And the comparison is unusually clean, because our Mountain bike and theirs are the same bike:

Same product, same price, same judgment scores, **2.1x the share**. There is no product explanation and no pricing explanation left. The entire gap is execution, which means all of it is copyable.

## The changes

### Manufacturing — the biggest single fix, and it's free

1. **Schedule operating capacity at 24/day**, up from 8. At 74% productivity over 65 days that's **1,154 units**, which covers the 1,171-unit plan forecast within 1.4%. Under-scheduling also drove labour cost per unit from $130 to $205, so right-sizing cuts unit cost *and* ends the stock-outs.
2. **Overtime 0.00 — do not copy BB LLC's 6.90.** Manufacturing Productivity is utilisation of scheduled capacity minus half the overtime ratio. BB LLC's overtime scores **0.856**; scheduling 24 with zero overtime scores **1.000**. Copy SpaceBikes on this one line (32 owned, 30 scheduled, zero OT) and BB LLC on everything else.
3. **Buy no printers.** We already own what the leader owns. The $148,652 excess-capacity charge in Q2 came from *scheduling* unused capacity, not owning it. A printer is only needed if we add NYC.

### Products — one fix, one deliberate non-fix

4. **Swift Bike gears: 24-speed → 14-speed (2×7).** +3 judgment. Swift Bike is the *only* Speed brand in the industry running a 24-speed mountain drivetrain — it inherited Hike Bike's. The simpler drivetrain should also cut unit cost.
5. **Swift Bike: add colourful thin brushstroke decals.** +2 judgment. Together these take Swift Bike **72 → 77**, tying the industry Speed ceiling and **beating BB LLC's Blu Tube at 76**.
6. **Hike Bike: no change.** 73 is the ceiling, tied with BB LLC's own bike.

### Pricing

7. **Swift Bike $1,450 → $1,580.** That is BB LLC's exact Speed price, and it draws **zero** Speed price resistance — proven by Blu Aero, Blu Tube and The Armstrong all sitting at $1,580 with judgment 100. Resistance only begins at $1,749. Contribution per unit goes $756 → **$886**.
8. **Hike Bike: hold $1,365.** Identical to BB LLC's Mountain price, and tier 81 is the highest tier still scoring 100 in Mountain.
9. **Priority: Hike Bike 1, Swift Bike 2 — unchanged.** Same pattern BB LLC uses. Once capacity matches the forecast, priority stops rationing anything.

### Advertising

10. **Rebuild the Swift Bike ad.** Target 70 → 77. Seven benefits, in this order:

    1. Mention brand name — fills the Ad/Brand columns without consuming an organic snippet slot
    2. Ride a wind-cheater — **8 of 8** rival Speed ads run this; we were missing it
    3. Roll fast with racing tires — 7 of 8 rivals, and the bike genuinely has them
    4. Elite look, a ride of distinction — 6 of 8, including both 77-point ads
    5. A tailor-made bike just for you (3D printing) — on both best ads; we actually 3D-print
    6. Local sales & service — we're 80.9% store and never claimed it
    7. Picture of road race — the one thing Q3 got right, ranked lower so text fills the snippet

    **Drop lights, reflectors, and "carbon fiber quality at a great price."** Zero of eight rival Speed ads run any of those; ours ran all three. Don't advertise a great price in the quarter we raise the price to $1,580. Preview the score before committing.

11. **Try to push HikeBike 1 past 82** by adding "Highest rated Mountain bike" and "Local sales & service." This reverses the earlier "leave it alone" call, and here's why: the Mountain organic SERP ranks **exactly in ad-judgment order** (81 > 80 > 79 > 78 > 77), and click-through is a step function on position — 22.8% at #1 versus 11.1% at #2. Beating BB Big Momma's 81 moves us from position 2 to position 1, worth **115 → 237 clicks on a page we already pay $1,000 for**. Both claims are legal (we're tied at 73) and BB LLC runs both. One point of ad judgment is cheap; 122 organic clicks is not. **Preview first — if it comes in below 80, keep the Q3 copy untouched.**
12. **Do not add a second ad for either brand.** BB LLC runs 2 ads for 4 brands and leads. Bike Bros runs 5 for 5 and is last — their two Speed pages cannibalise each other into positions 7 and 8.

### Store staffing — we can clone BB LLC's crew exactly

BB LLC runs the identical template in both stores: **1 Service / 0 Recreation / 3 Mountain / 3 Speed = 7.** Seven per city is a hard cap; no firm anywhere exceeds it.

20. **Amsterdam: train the untrained person into Speed.** We're at the cap (7), so training is the only legal move. Result: 1 Service / 3 Mountain / 3 Speed — an exact BB LLC clone. Cost **$400**.
21. **Rio: train the untrained person into Mountain.** Only Bike Bros, in last place, also carries untrained staff; the other five firms train everyone.
22. **Rio: hire 3 — one Mountain, two Speed.** Result: 1 Service / 3 Mountain / 3 Speed — the second exact clone, and total store force **11 → 14, matching BB LLC's 14**. Rio measures **60 units per head** (we're the only store in the city) against Amsterdam's 38, so this is the highest-return staffing move available: roughly **+180 store units**.

### Compensation — BB LLC leads both productivity scores in the industry

23. **Sales: $22,000 salary / Expanded coverage / 2 weeks / 4% pension = $27,402.**
24. **Production: $18,500 salary / Expanded / 2 weeks / 4% = $23,042.**

Both of our gaps are salary plus the Full-coverage mistake. The proof that mix beats total: **MILC pays more than BB LLC on sales ($27,630 vs $27,402) and scores 8.4 satisfaction points worse**, because they use Full coverage and 1 week of vacation. Our sales package is a milder version of the same error — Full coverage wrapped around a low salary and a 1% pension. Salary is the top-rated lever (importance 87).

25. **Keep the planner at 74% productivity. Never 85%.** Q3 planned 85% and delivered 70/72. BB LLC's 78% sales productivity is the industry ceiling. Pay buys a few HR points, not fifteen productivity points.

### Everything else

26. **Issue no stock.** $1,010,838 already sits idle — that's exactly what Asset Management 0.353 punishes.
27. **Target segments: Mountain primary, Speed secondary.** Already identical to BB LLC. No action, just don't touch it.
28. **Organic SEM: 2 pages at $1,000 each, unchanged.** BB LLC gets 437 clicks from the *same* two pages and the *same* $2,000. Rank is the gap, not budget — and since rank follows ad judgment, changes 10 and 11 *are* the SEM fix.
29. **No rebates.** Only the two Recreation price leaders use them.
30. **No Recreation brand — park it for Q5.** BB LLC doesn't have one scoring 70+ either. Brand count doesn't drive share: Bike Bros has 5 brands and finished last, SpaceBikes has 2 and leads cumulatively.

## What we deliberately do not copy

- **BB LLC's 6.90 overtime.** Costs Manufacturing Productivity (0.856 vs 1.000) and we don't need it.
- **BB LLC's 4 brands.** Their third and fourth brands run *unadvertised* as overflow catchers at a higher price. That only works with spare capacity. File for Q5.
- **BB LLC's NYC store.** Tier 2. They hold 82.8% of NYC Mountain, and entering needs a printer plus seven new hires. If we go, go via **Speed** — 880 units, no dominant firm, Spoke'd Up's Speed ad is under 70 — not Mountain. Depth in Rio and the web pays back faster.
- **Bike Bros' breadth.** Most ads and most brands in the industry, and last place.
- **MILC's compensation.** Highest sales spend, worse satisfaction. Mix beats total.
